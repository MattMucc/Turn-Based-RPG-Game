## Accepted battle foundation
- Full grid with eight-direction movement, one point per adjacent cell including diagonals, and no walking through blocked corners. User approved this baseline rather than chess-like traversal restrictions. Prototype world scale is 2 metres per horizontal cell and 1 metre per elevation level; verify scale visually before treating it as final. Ranged line of sight and accuracy remain open.
- Six attributes: Vitality, Strength, Magic, Physical Defense, Magical Defense, Initiative. Vitality is intended to support maximum HP; exact scaling/formulas remain open. Movement is NOT a leveled attribute. Each character gains THREE movement points at the start of their turn, added to their own remaining balance from the previous turn. No carryover cap approved; prototype has none. Future effects may suppress/modify the grant.
- Strength should determine jump height, with a baseline of one elevation level. Exact thresholds, caps and whether temporary Strength modifies traversal are undecided. Do not remove Strength-based jumping without discussion.
- Movement budget, horizontal jump range and Strength-based vertical clearance are separate. Accepted baseline: horizontal range 2 cells, vertical clearance 1 level. A one-cell gap needs a 2-cell jump costing 2 movement; a two-cell gap needs range 3 and costs 3 movement. Jumping spends movement, never an additional action. Strength thresholds are not implemented yet.
- Initial elements: Fire, Lightning, Ice. Initial statuses: Burning, CHILLED (user replaced Frozen), Paralyzed, Weakened, Exposed, Regeneration, Barrier. Chilled reduces movement; amount, stacking and duration remain open. Paralyzed prevents dodging and parrying. Do not infer either status skips turns.
- Burning: stacks represent remaining ticks; at a burn tick, apply burn damage then remove ONE stack. One stack produces one tick; three stacks survive for three ticks unless modified. More applications add stacks. Tick damage formula, source attribution, caps and whether damage also scales with stack count are not finalized; do not assume all stacks are consumed at once.
- Skills can apply multiple stacks on a successful status application. A landed hit is required before rolling a conditional on-hit status chance. Status application probability is separate from hit accuracy. Example explicitly requested: base 10% plus equipment bonus of 5 percentage points gives 15%; display base and modifiers, e.g. `10% (+5%)`, with a clear total and source breakdown. Per-hit vs per-action rolls for multi-hit skills remain open.
- Full carried consumable inventory is available in combat; no separate battle loadout/cap. Using an item costs an action. Example 99 items illustrates access to carried quantity, not a confirmed maximum stack size. Storage should be generous; exact capacity remains open.
- Initial terrain: one hazard and one obstacle. Ranged accuracy based on distance/elevation is under consideration, not approved as a formula. Melee access to distant/elevated enemies needs deliberate encounter design.
- Three active party members; visible order and one turn per combatant per round; Initiative orders turns rather than granting extra turns. Movement NEVER consumes the main action, including longer movement. Movement may occur before and after an action. Actions include attacks, skills, Block/Defend and items. Taking an action does not end the turn. Explicit End Turn control permits ending with unused movement, which carries over. Party switching's previously proposed action cost is still a baseline for later integration. Reactive dodge/parry does not consume the next turn; reliable Block mitigation and explicit timing assistance are desired.
- Other accepted initial recommendations: physical/magical damage classification separate from elemental affinity and applied statuses; explicit skill scaling; no permanent character elemental weaknesses initially; AoE/multi-hit support with controlled trigger frequency; designed breakable parts on selected enemies; later shared synergy gauge with one initial team action before adding tiers; crafting deferred; cinematic QTE outcomes explicitly defined.
- User explicitly intends future statuses, attacks or skills that prevent a turn. Support this in the eventual turn lifecycle; exact skip behavior, status ticking and debt collection during skipped turns remain open. Preserve the agreed opportunity to handle due debt when defining those interactions rather than silently choosing a policy. Status lifecycle must prevent a newly created effect expiring accidentally in the same processing pass.
- User authorized starting the battle-grid prototype. An isolated traversal/action-budget sandbox is now implemented (see below); the full encounter loop and character kits remain future work.

## Grid prototype implementation
- Standalone scene: `Assets/Scenes/GridPrototype.unity`. It remains a separate sandbox. SampleScene's overworld encounter path is now integrated with the same grid model (see below).
- Runtime: `Assets/Scripts/GridPrototype/`. `GridBattleModel.cs` contains independent movement/action budgets, per-unit carryover, path search, eight-way traversal, occupancy, water jumps and elevation checks. `GridPrototypeArena.cs` creates the 10x8 test arena, three manually controlled units, route highlights, movement animation and temporary UI.
- Controls: short left click (on release) previews a cell, click again confirms; arrows select and Enter confirms. M selects Move, R selects practice Attack (A is reserved for camera flight), B spends action on Block, T ends turn. Controller: D-pad selects, South confirms, West toggles Move/Attack, North blocks, Start ends turn. Explicit Move, Attack, Block, Confirm, End Turn and Focus buttons. No auto-end even when budgets are exhausted.
- User requested continuous terrain independent of the tactical grid. `GridTerrainView.cs` now generates one continuous terrain mesh/collider with separate white grid geometry and translucent green reachable-cell overlays; unreachable terrain retains its original surface appearance. Selected cell gets an outline. This is procedural prototype geometry, not an imported environment asset or a finished terrain-authoring workflow.
- `GridCameraController.cs`: perspective camera; WASD free flight, Q/E local down/up, Shift faster, right-drag look, left-drag pan, wheel moves along viewing direction, F/button focuses active character, middle-drag orbits. Focus locks orbit to active figure; flight/pan/dolly/free look releases that lock. Unfocused orbit picks the center-view ray hit, or a point at remembered depth when looking at empty space. No camera collision constraint yet.
- Left-drag never selects/confirms a grid destination; 6-pixel drag threshold, click accepted on release only. Camera gestures started over the UI are ignored; wheel over UI scrolls the panel. Camera remains usable while units animate. Losing application focus cancels gestures.
- Pathfinding preserves movement costs and legality but breaks equal-cost ties by geometric route length, avoiding unnecessary zigzags on clear straight routes.
- Sandbox includes a one-cell river and two-cell gap, elevations 0/1/2, solid obstacle, practice target and orange hazard. Default jump range 2 cannot cross the two-cell gap even with saved movement. Solid obstacles are not vaultable in this first version. Vertical traversal currently limits descent as well as ascent to JumpHeight; falling is not implemented.
- Practice attack deals 10 displayed damage to an adjacent stationary dummy. Hazard deals 10 per entered tile, 5 while blocking, and cannot KO sandbox units. These are temporary test behaviors, not balanced combat rules. All three units are manually controlled in fixed order; no enemy AI or Initiative sorting yet.
- Standalone prototype does NOT implement character kits, stat scaling, skills, inventory/item use, statuses, dodge/parry, cinematic QTEs or victory/defeat. Same-scene encounter integration is now separate below. Unused actions do not carry over. Turn-skip/debt lifecycle policy is still open; `BeginTurn(0)` permits testing a suppressed movement grant without losing saved points.
- Builder menu: `Tools > Tenebrasia > Create Grid Prototype Scene`; creates only when absent, preserves the current scene, never overwrites an existing prototype.
- Tests: `Assets/Tests/GridPrototype/EditMode/`, assembly `Tenebrasia.Grid.Tests`; 22 EditMode tests passed covering budgets, carryover, corners, occupancy, range, height, hazards, straight-route regression, click/drag discrimination and camera focus/orbit. ## Same-scene overworld encounter integration
- User requested overworld enemy contact to enter a grid on nearby existing terrain with no scene change. Implemented via `BattleGroup.TriggerBattle` -> `BattleManager.TryStartBattle` -> `InSceneGridBattle`; no scene loads or terrain replacement occur.
- `TerrainBattleGrid` samples a 14x12 region by default (2m cells), centered near the possessed player. It tests center/corner support, slope (35 degrees), capsule clearance using participating CharacterController dimensions, and intermediate movement clearance. One surface per X/Z cell; no layered bridge/cave selection or dynamic terrain resampling yet. Static colliders required. Water layer surfaces are jumpable gaps; missing support is blocked, not assumed to be water.
- Starting cells are unique, close to actors' world positions, in the player's connected walkable component. All existing active enemies from the triggering group participate. Entry is rejected before actors move if the full group cannot fit safely. No synthetic extra party members: currently uses one possessed player because no party roster exists yet.
- Optional `Grid Center` transform on BattleGroup provides an authored same-scene encounter center. Grid dimensions/cell size configured on BattleManager. Old fixed battle-position fields are retained for prefab compatibility but are no longer used to place actors.
- `SampledGridOverlay` renders only outlines/reachable fills/route over original geometry. `InSceneGridBattle` uses original actor transforms and a temporary camera with the established free-fly controls. World player movement, camera rotation and roaming AI pause. Participating colliders/rigidbodies are suspended; snapshots restore position, rotation, activation, collider/body states. Original camera is disabled temporarily, not moved. Input and cursor return to overworld state on exit.
- Minimal encounter loop: one player followed by living group enemies; placeholder 100 HP/25 attack, Block reduces to 12. Enemies approach legal melee cells and attack. No Initiative sorting, timed defense, party switching, statuses, finalized character kits, full inventory or rewards yet; demonstration action/item targeting is described below. Player movement/actions and carryover match the prototype.
- Leave button/defeat reset all actors to pre-battle positions and restore surviving/deactivated enemy models. Victory removes the triggering group's enemies. Five-second re-entry cooldown; failed placement retries after three seconds. Trigger-stay supports later retry if actors remain touching.
- SampleScene explicitly references the overlay shader on BattleManager. Standalone GridPrototype scene still works independently.
- Verification: 31 EditMode tests passed after integration (existing tests plus 9 terrain/placement tests); restoration tested separately. Compilation clean. NO Play Mode or runtime integration playtest performed; user owns playtesting. Editor remained unfocused during checks.

## Design process
1. Choose the common battle format: positioning, active party size, turn/action economy and timed defense.
2. Establish minimal stats/resources, damage/elements/status rules and equipment scope that support that format.
3. Design a small sample encounter including misses, defeat, recovery and reliable end-of-turn debt timing.
4. After agreeing on the baseline, implement a small playable battle, then develop and implement character kits one at a time.
5. Expand gear, progression, enemy mechanics and cinematic content as the battle prototype demonstrates a need.
6. Return to backstories/world/story later at the user's direction; they are currently on hold.
